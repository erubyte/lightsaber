Get Started

Safety notice

Charge the saber to full before you first use it. Charge it only with the included cable and a 5V 1A USB charger. A stronger charger can damage the battery and electronics.

Don't leave the saber charging unattended, and unplug it when it's full.

Warning

Keep it away from young children. Older children use it with an adult present.

Duel with control and respect. Don't aim at faces, and stop if the blade cracks.

Anatomy

BladeA polycarbonate tube with Neopixel LED strips. It's 81 cm long.
EmitterThe top of the hilt. Two screws hold the blade in it.
HiltThe metal handle. It's 31 cm long and unscrews at the joint and at the pommel.
CoreThe removable electronics: board, battery and speaker. It sits in the lower half of the hilt (page 23).
PommelThe bottom cap. Sound comes out through its holes, and it unscrews to take the core out.

How it fits together

A saber is a blade, a hilt and a core. The core slides out of the hilt, and the board inside it controls the light and the sound.

Blade

A polycarbonate tube with two LED strips inside, back to back. Each LED holds its own red, green and blue light and a tiny chip, so it can show any color on its own. The board sends a stream of colors up the strips and changes them to make the blade grow from the hilt or flicker like fire.

Pogo pins

Spring-loaded contacts at both joints, so the blade and the core connect without plugs or wires.

Hilt

The metal shell. It holds the button and the wires between the two joints. The rest of the electronics sit in the core.

Core

One removable unit: board, battery, speaker, charging port and accent LEDs (page 23).

The board is the circuit board inside the core, also called the sound board. It runs the software, so it sets the sounds, blade effects and controls. Lightsabers are typically named after the board type they use; a Proffie saber is a lightsaber with a Proffieboard core.

Quick start

Charge to full (page 10). The first charge takes a few hours.

Push the blade all the way into the emitter.

Tighten the two 5 mm screws with the hex key until snug.

Click Power to ignite. Hold Power to turn off. Long Click to try the other presets.

< 9 >

Charging

Hold the grip and turn the pommel at the bottom counterclockwise to unscrew it.

Pull the pommel off.

Tilt the hilt so its open bottom points down. The gold core slides out, speaker end first.

Plug a USB-C cable into the port next to the gold pins. Use a 5V 1A charger only. The light by the speaker shows red while charging and green when full.

Slide the core back in, gold pins first, then screw the pommel back on.

The hilt also unscrews at the joint below the crystal chamber, which frees the core from the top instead.

< 10 >

Crystal cover

The silver sleeve around the crystal chamber turns, so the window shows either the crystals or the black back of the chamber. Quinlan Vos's hilt in The Clone Wars has no window, so the closed look matches the show.

Find the sleeve screw on the back of the hilt, behind the crystal window (page 6, part 9).

Loosen it about half a turn with the hex key. Don't take it out.

Turn the silver sleeve until the window closes, or opens again. Don't force it.

Tighten the screw.

Common errors

A robot voice names each problem the board finds. The voice is built into the board, so it works even when the SD card can't be read. It sounds muffled and clipped.

It saysMeaningFix
“Font directory not found”The board can't find a preset's sound folder on the SD card, or can't read the card right now. A loose blade causes it too. It says this once for each missing folderPush the blade all the way in and tighten both blade screws. Then charge, restart the core and check the folder names match the presets
“SD card not found”The board can't see the card at allRestart the core. The card must be FAT32
“Error in font directory”A font has missing or misnumbered files, or the menu voice files are goneCopy the font and the common folder back
“Voice pack not found”The common folder with the menu voice is missingCopy common back onto the card
“Error in voice pack version”The common folder is too old for ProffieOS 8Install the newer voice pack
“Error in blade array”The board doesn't detect the blade it expectsPush the blade all the way in, then restart
“Low battery”The battery is running downCharge it

To restart the core, slide it out and back in, or click its power switch off and on (page 23).

A saber set up without the voice plays a short tune instead, one note per syllable of the same messages. Hear each one at pod.hubbe.net/troubleshooting/beep_codes.html.

Troubleshooting

Saber won't turn on

Charge it. Check the core went back in speaker end first, and screw the pommel on tight so the gold pins touch.

Saber says “Font directory not found”

Push the blade all the way in and tighten both blade screws (page 6). A loose blade loses contact with the pogo pins, so the board can't identify it.

The saber speaks an error

Look it up under Common errors (page 18).

No sound

Turn the volume up (Hold Power + Clash, blade off).

Presses do the wrong thing

Check the blade angle (page 13). Long Click does different things pointing up and down.

Saber turns on when you move it

Gestures are on. Hold Power + Clash pointing down to turn them off.

Blade flickers or only half lights

Push the blade all the way into the emitter and tighten the two retention screws.

Customization

How the core works

The board is a small computer. Many times a second it reads the motion sensor and the button, works out what happened, then updates the blade and the sound.

Light

A blade style gives each LED a color, and the board works the colors out again for each frame. An ignition is a style that lights the LEDs from the hilt up.

Sound

Sounds are WAV files on the SD card. The board mixes up to five at once, such as the hum, a swing and a clash, and plays them through the speaker.

Effects

A hit, a stab or a press gives the board an event. The board passes it to the blade style, which flashes the blade, and to the sound font, which plays the clash, in the same frame.

Presets

A preset pairs one sound font with one blade style. Switching presets changes the whole saber at once.

You can change each of these in software (page 30).

< 28 >

What is Proffie

Proffie is this saber's board and the software it runs: the Proffieboard and ProffieOS. Fredrik Hübinette designed both, and the name comes from his nickname, Profezzorn.

Anyone can read, change and share ProffieOS under the GPLv3 license, and Fredrik publishes the board's design files. You can reprogram this saber yourself.

Where things live
WhereWhatChange it
Firmware, in the processorYour config file: blade styles, button logic, featuresUpload from a computer
SD cardSound fonts, music, quotes, menu voiceCopy files
Saved settings, on the SD cardEdit Mode changes: font, color, timingsOn the saber
From config to saber

You edit the config as plain text. A compiler on your computer turns it into firmware, and the upload replaces the program on the processor. Fonts and settings on the SD card stay as they are.

ProffieOS 8 came out in 2026 and runs on this 2019 board. Updates stay free.

What you can change

Start at the top. Each step needs more tools and can change more.

On the saber

Pick a preset, then change its color, sound font, blade length, ignition speed or volume in Edit Mode (page 31). Record a fight to replay with Rehearsal (page 32).

On the SD card

Add sound fonts, music to play along with, or quotes. Replace one sound, such as the hum, with any WAV file of your own (page 33).

In the code

Write new blade effects, such as fire, an unstable blade or a rainbow. Change what each press does, turn gestures on or off, or give Quinlan Vos a light and a dark font that swap as he turns (pages 34 to 39).

In the hardware

Add Bluetooth so Workbench connects without a cable, or more accent LEDs. These need a soldering iron (page 41).

Rehearsal

Record a fight sequence, then replay it as choreography.

Blade off: Triple Click and Hold. The saber ignites in Rehearsal mode. If you saved a rehearsal before, it asks Replace? Turn right and Click to start a new one.

Fight your sequence. The saber records each Clash and Hold + Clash (lockup), with its sound.

Hold Power to save. Triple Click and Hold cancels.

Blade off: Hold Power + Swing to play it back. Clashes and lockups replay in order.

Hold Power to end.

Connect a computer

Open the gold core and plug into the micro-USB port on the Proffieboard. The USB-C port on the core only charges. For a computer with small oval ports, get a USB-C to Micro-USB cable; for wide rectangular ports, USB-A to Micro-USB.

The cable must carry data. Stores list it as a data or sync cable. Charge-only cables look the same and won't work. Cables from phones and game controllers tend to carry data, and ones from lamps and fans often don't. If nothing appears on the computer, try another cable.

< 34 >

Back up first

Save three things before changing anything. Each one lets you undo a mistake.

Copy the whole SD card to your computer, including the .ini and .tmp files. They hold your Edit Mode changes.

Find the config file: a .h file on the card, or inside a ProffieOS zip. If it's missing, ask DamienSaber for it. The board can't give it back.

Save the firmware. In Chrome, open profezzorn.github.io/webdfu/dfu-util, send RebootDFU over serial, click Connect, then Read.

To undo Edit Mode changes, delete presets.ini and presets.tmp from the card, then restart the saber.

You can recover from a bad upload. Hold BOOT, press RESET, then let go of BOOT, and the processor's built-in bootloader takes new firmware.

Set up the code

Clone the code: git clone github.com/profezzorn/ProffieOS, or download release 8.10 or later.

Install the Arduino IDE or arduino-cli. Add the board URL below, then install the Proffieboard plugin.

Choose Proffieboard V2. Set USB Type to Serial + Mass Storage + WebUSB and DOSFS to SDCARD (SPI).

Put the config in ProffieOS/config and set CONFIG_FILE in ProffieOS.ino to its name.

Your first change

Start from a build that matches the factory one, then change one thing at a time.

Build and upload the factory config unchanged. The saber should behave as before.

Make one small edit, such as a color in the first preset or an ignition time from 300 to 600 ms.

Upload and test it with the blade on.

Commit the config to git, so you keep each working version.

Stuck? Ask on The Crucible, crucible.hubbe.net. Fredrik and Fett263 answer questions there.

A blade style is code that sets the color of each LED, many times a second. Styles stack in layers. Even Green is a style, so you can swap any color for an effect.

< 39 >

Under the hood

SmoothSwing

Two looping swing sounds play at zero volume under the hum. Swing speed sets their volume, and the board fades from one to the other as the blade turns. The hum dips as you swing.

Clash detection

A clash is a jolt that doesn't match how the saber is moving. Sound from the speaker shakes the sensor too, so ProffieOS raises the threshold as you turn the volume up.

|accel - down|/2 + |delta gyro|/2 > threshold

STM32L433 at 80 MHz, 256 KB flash, 64 KB RAM. ProffieOS has no operating system kernel. Its tasks take turns in one loop.

What fits what

A Xenopixel core fits this hilt

Padawan Outpost sells this model with a Xenopixel V3 or a Proffie V2.2 core. Both are the same pogo core size and drive the same blade.

Xenopixel and Proffie share blades

Any 1 in Neopixel blade with a three-ring contact disc works with either board.

A baselit core needs another emitter

A baselit core shines its own LED up a hollow blade, and this emitter holds the pin connector for a Neopixel blade.

A baselit blade stays dark

It fits, but a hollow blade has no LEDs, and this hilt has no LED to shine into it.

Other pogo cores need the same format

The core must be 1 in wide, with the same length and pin layout. LGT, Nexus and DamienSaber hilts share it. Check with the seller for other brands.

You can swap the board alone

It takes soldering. The board comes out of the core, and the battery and speaker stay.

Your settings stay with the old core

Fonts and presets live on its SD card and board. Copy the card first (page 35).

A store that lists a core for Baselit, Xenopixel V3 and Proffie V2.2 hilts sells three versions, one per board. For this saber, choose the Proffie V2.2 version. Padawan Outpost lists this hilt as PO x KR core compatible.

Glossary

Config fileThe C++ file that describes this saber: blade, button, presets and features.
CoreBoard, battery and speaker in one removable unit that slides into the hilt.
NeopixelA blade lined with LED strips in which each LED shows its own color.
Pogo pinsSpring-loaded contacts that connect the blade and the core to the hilt without plugs.
PresetOne saved setup: a sound font, a blade style and a music track.
Prop fileThe part of ProffieOS that turns presses and gestures into actions. This saber uses the Fett263 prop.
ProffieboardThe sound board in this core. This one is V2.2, and it runs the open-source ProffieOS.
Serial monitorA text window for typing commands to the saber over USB.
